
# Bitcoin Cash (BCH) — Comprehensive Research Report

## Executive Summary

**Bitcoin Cash (BCH)** is a cryptocurrency that forked from Bitcoin in August 2017. It was created by a faction of the Bitcoin community who wanted larger block sizes to enable faster, cheaper transactions, positioning BCH as "peer-to-peer electronic cash" rather than just a store of value.

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Launched** | August 1, 2017 (Block 478,559) |
| **Symbol/Ticker** | BCH |
| **Max Supply** | 21 million (same as Bitcoin) |
| **Block Time** | 10 minutes |
| **Block Size** | 32 MB (vs Bitcoin's 1-4 MB) |
| **Consensus** | SHA-256 Proof-of-Work |
| **Current Supply** | ~19.77 million BCH |

## The Origin Story: Why Bitcoin Cash Was Created

### The Scaling Debate
Since Bitcoin's inception, a debate raged within the community about how to scale the network:

**Small Block Proponents** (Bitcoin Core):
- Keep blocks small (~1 MB)
- Use second-layer solutions like Lightning Network for scalability
- Position Bitcoin as "digital gold" — a store of value

**Big Block Proponents** (Bitcoin Cash):
- Increase block size to handle more transactions
- Keep transactions on-chain
- Position Bitcoin as "peer-to-peer electronic cash" for daily payments

### The Fork
On **August 1, 2017**, the Bitcoin blockchain split at block 478,559:
- Anyone holding 1 BTC received 1 BCH
- Bitcoin Cash activated with 8 MB blocks (later increased to 32 MB in 2018)
- The fork was contentious and marked the beginning of the "Bitcoin Civil War"

**Key Figures:**
- **Roger Ver** ("Bitcoin Jesus") — Major proponent
- **Jihan Wu** (Bitmain) — Provided mining support
- **Gavin Andresen** — Former Bitcoin lead developer endorsed BCH

## Key Technical Differences from Bitcoin

| Feature | Bitcoin (BTC) | Bitcoin Cash (BCH) |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Block Size** | 1-4 MB (with SegWit) | 32 MB |
| **Transactions/Second** | ~7 TPS | ~100+ TPS |
| **Average Fee** | $1-50+ (varies) | <$0.01 |
| **Transaction Speed** | 10-60 minutes | Seconds to minutes |
| **Scaling Approach** | Layer 2 (Lightning) | On-chain (big blocks) |
| **Primary Use Case** | Store of value | Medium of exchange |

### Key Features of Bitcoin Cash

1. **Low Fees**: Transaction fees typically under $0.01
2. **Fast Confirmations**: Most transactions confirm within seconds to minutes
3. **Reliable Network**: Zero downtime since 2017
4. **32 MB Blocks**: Can handle significantly more volume than Bitcoin
5. **CashTokens (2023)**: Native support for fungible and non-fungible tokens
6. **Adaptive Block Limit Algorithm (ABLA)**: Automatically scales block size with demand

## Internal Conflicts & Further Forks

Bitcoin Cash itself has experienced contentious forks:

### November 2018: Bitcoin SV Split
- **Bitcoin SV (BSV)** — Led by Craig Wright
- Dispute over block size (BCH wanted 32 MB, SV wanted 128 MB+)
- "Satoshi's Vision" claimed to be the true Bitcoin
- Bitmain/Roger Ver vs. Craig Wright/Calvin Ayre

### November 2020: eCash (XEC) Split
- **Bitcoin ABC** team forked to create BCHA (later renamed eCash)
- Dispute over mining rewards (8% developer tax proposed)
- Majority of network stayed with BCH

## Criticism & Controversies

### Centralization Concerns
- Bitmain's dominance in mining (2017-2018)
- Roger Ver's influence and marketing
- Concentration of hashpower

### "Bcash" Naming Controversy
- Detractors call it "Bcash," "Btrash," or "a scam"
- Supporters call it "the true Bitcoin"
- Twitter @Bitcoin account controversy (suspended for supporting BCH)

### Volatility & Performance
- Down 88% from all-time high (as of 2018)
- Failed to maintain payment adoption momentum
- Ongoing competition from other payment cryptocurrencies

### Security Incidents
- 2018: Critical bug found by Bitcoin Core developer Cory Fields (patched before exploitation)
- Concerns about fewer developers than Bitcoin Core

## Current State (2024-2025)

### Strengths
1. **Reliable, low-cost transactions**
2. **Established brand recognition**
3. **Strong merchant payment infrastructure**
4. **Multiple development teams (decentralized)**
5. **CashTokens enabling DeFi/NFT capabilities**

2. **Declined from #3 to outside top 10 by market cap**
3. **Less developer activity than Ethereum/Solana**
4. **Competition from Lightning Network and stablecoins**

- **Active Addresses**: Declining trend
- **Market Cap**: ~$5-10 billion range
